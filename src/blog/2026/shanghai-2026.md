This was not only my first time in Shanghai, but my first time in Mainland China! I went in equal parts excited and slightly intimidated — mostly because I knew I’d have to figure out the whole WeChat + Alipay ecosystem on the fly. We arrived late on Wednesday and immediately ran into the first “oh, we’re not in Taiwan anymore” moment: most restaurants near us were already closed by 8 or 8:30.

Thankfully, a close-by Maotou Laodie 毛头老爹饭店 was still open. There were a surprising number of foreigners inside which made me nervous at first (that the food might be meh), but once the dishes came out all was forgotten and everything was honestly really good. Portions were HUGE, prices were low, and we weren't even close to finishing everything we ordered.

Afterwards, we also made it to Chagee at 1788 Plaza before they closed for the night, making for a very pleasant first evening.

Thursday was a classic first-timer itinerary: Yu Garden + the Bund. Upon arriving, it was almost noon, so we tried looking for something to eat first. Unfortunately the area around Yu Garden felt _extremely_ touristy (as one would expect) and most food options looked unappetizing. We ended up trying Bistro10 (a hotel restaurant off the Bund), and it was a mixed bag: the xiaolongbao was actually really good and flavorful, but the crab roe noodles were super bland and definitely not worth the price.

What's funny was that since it was our first time having crab roe noodles, we kinda assumed that maybe that's just how it was, so we weren't even that disappointed after the meal. Afterwards, we headed to Yu Garden, which was breathtakingly beautiful and surprisingly serene without the weekend crowd rush. Eventually, we emerged to grab an afternoon refresher, and I found the famed Amo Shouzuo tucked away amidst the tourist shops. The wait was around 20 minutes, which I learned later is basically lightning-fast by Amo standards... and the mochi drink was seriously no joke - strong contender for one of the best drinks I've had in my lifetime.

Late afternoon was spent strolling along a foggy Bund and weaving through crowds on Nanjing Road. Overcome by a sudden craving for spicy food, we found good ratings on Dianping for a place called Shu Tan Ji 蜀谭记 and headed over with high hopes. The first sign that it was good was that there was quite a long wait outside from locals, and it was about 7:30PM at that point, which apparently is quite late for a dinner in Shanghai! The second sign that it was good was that when the Sichuan boiling fish 沸騰魚 came out, the bowl was wider than the width of my body.

Upon having my first bite, it felt like heaven on earth. The flavors were _so_ bold and fragrant, I couldn't believe my taste buds. They were also very generous on the portion of fish - most of the time you order boiling fish 水煮魚 (which I recognize is not quite the same) in the U.S., you end up getting heaping spoonfuls of beansprouts and stray vegetables, but here, every time the ladle went in and out, more pieces of fish were discovered. It was honestly one of the best meals I've ever had.

The Bund at night afterwards ended up being one of my favorite Shanghai moments: good vibes, not crowded, super scenic, although way windier than expected (bring a light outer layer!). Also got some pretty good photos.

Friday was more food + shopping, which is basically my ideal travel rhythm. Yang’s Dumpling for shengjianbao in the morning (meat was the clear winner; shrimp was solid; xiaolongbao were fine but not the star), then Shangshan Teahouse milk tea after. I also bought a cute duck keychain (yay!).

Later, I redeemed the Songmont situation by going to the flagship store on Huaihai right around opening time — a _much_ better experience than Rockbund. Staff were helpful, it wasn’t crowded, and I walked out with two bags. They also mentioned a 15% discount, which made me even more annoyed that the Rockbund store didn’t say anything (like… hello??).

And yes, we did Matcha Wang for the sheep matcha photo. It was mostly foreigners and the drink was just alright, but watching them make it was fun and the wait wasn’t too bad on a weekday. Julu Road → Anfu Road ended up being one of my favorite parts of Shanghai: cute boutique shops, indie designer brands, trees, cafés, and generally very good vibes. Unfortunately, everything was also expensive, so I mostly just looked. I passed by a store called Monday Sleeping Club which I thought was kind of cute but a bit overpriced so I didn’t buy (more on this later).

_One random practical learning from being out all day: Meituan (yellow) charging banks don’t work without a China phone number, so we had to hunt down the Energy Monster (blue) ones instead._

Friday night, Lailai Xiaolong Jing’an was another highlight. The crab roe rice was genuinely so much better than the crab roe noodles from Bistro10 (redemption!), and their pork chop was surprisingly delicious too. XLB were decent, wonton was just okay and a bit small, but overall it was super worth it and also really affordable. Blueglass yogurt after was pretty good considering I don’t usually like yogurt drinks.

Saturday was supposed to be Zhujiajiao Water Town day trip, but we pivoted to Suzhou instead (after a morning-of Reddit scrolling session). Shanghai Railway Station was a little chaotic, and we found out most of the HSR tickets were sold out — the earliest option was about 1.5 hours later. So… buy tickets in advance if you can. The wait wasn’t too bad though: early lunch in the station (surprisingly good xiaolongbao, _super_ cheap), Chagee (predictable), and then boarding.

_Also: if you’re a non-resident ID holder, you need to go through the “manual entry” line for passport checks before boarding. Same idea when exiting, and you also have to go through security before you can even enter the boarding area._

The train to Suzhou was fast (25 minutes). The Didi to Humble Administrator’s Garden was not (30+ minutes because of traffic). Lesson learned: take the metro in crowded areas. The garden was pretty, but it was Saturday noon, extremely crowded, and absolutely sweltering — people everywhere, everyone taking photos, and it was chaotic enough that we left after about an hour. Perhaps it would’ve been more serene on a weekday, but that experience was truly a bit hellish.

Pingjiang Road was also insanely crowded, and the main area felt a bit too touristy. Things improved a lot once we wandered onto a quieter side street. We ordered Amo Shouzuo again and waited almost an hour this time (pain), but it was still good.

The best surprise was finding a cute Taiwanese cafe selling street snacks — we sat there for over an hour just chatting with the owners, and it was much quieter and more relaxed than everything else. Later, we walked by the river and saw this giant ancient wall with bridges, canals, and trees, and it felt _so_ much more beautiful (and definitely calmer) than the human-photographer-infested garden.

After Suzhou, we metro’d back to the railway station, HSR’d to Shanghai, and immediately went back to Anfu Lu for Monday Sleeping Club. (Yes… don’t ask.) I saw it the day before as aforementioned and thought it wasn’t all that - and then decided it was — so Daniel and I each got a t-shirt, and I got a hat as well.

To end the day, we did Xintiandi + hotpot at Jinshan Koufu, and it was one of the best hotpots I’ve ever had. It wasn’t super cheap (around ~$32 per person), but the meat quality was really good and everything was just honestly super delicious. We tried to get boba after and failed because it was 9:40, and apparently some places that “close at 10” stop taking orders at 9:30. We walked around Xintiandi afterwards and it felt very upscale Europe-outdoor-dining-street vibes (and also a little Las Vegas) which was a little strange in the center of Shanghai, but perhaps not as strange considering the city’s international history.

Overall, Shanghai was really fun and there’s a _lot_ to do, but three days felt short. There were places we didn’t get to (Tianzifang, People’s Square, Jing’an Temple), and it also would’ve been cool to stay longer in Suzhou or even go to Zhouzhuang (introduced to me by Chloe Gong) if we had more time!

It is a little intimidating having to use WeChat and Alipay everywhere, and some services just don’t work without a Chinese phone number (can’t order delivery on Meituan, can’t buy HSR tickets in advance on your phone). Things also close earlier than Taiwan, and there aren’t really night markets or street food stalls in the same way. But I’d definitely go back and visit again!

Favorite things:

- the Bund on a weekday night
- 沸騰魚 at Shu Tan Ji
- Songmont flagship
- Monday Sleeping Club
- Amo Shouzuo

Least favorite things:

- crowds at Humble Administrator’s Garden and Pingjiang Road
- crab roe noodles at Bistro10
- waiting one hour for Amo Shouzuo
- Didi drivers lowkey scamming us (telling us to adjust the dropoff location so that they get paid more, accepting a ride and then never showing up so we’re forced to cancel)
- Songmont Rockbund store
